Evaluation roadmap: rigorous recovery, controls and scaling

Prepared 14 September 2026 from repository state 0629ba9d600afcd0cdbf1ddfc30ef83dbe6088c4. The working tree contained the untracked owner file PROJECT_BRIEF.md; it was read for context and not changed. This document proposes the next stage. It does not amend the four frozen protocols, reinterpret their results, or authorize a new real-data run.

Executive recommendation

Do not add PyTorch now. Eka already has the important scientific foundation: composition-safe positive–unlabelled (PU) recovery, a whole-chemical-system holdout, three defensible references, a fixed element-pair factor comparator, label-sensitivity analysis, independent Python checks and exact local reruns. The highest-value next step is to make the evaluation layer reusable and system-aware, then add one direct observed-pair control and one prospectively specified missing-pair ablation.

Use chemical-system holdout as the primary generalisation protocol. Retain composition holdout as an interpolation control, not as the headline discovery test. Keep composition Hits@100 as the primary operational outcome because it answers “how many known positive-provenance formulas are recovered in a fixed screening budget?” Add macro Recall@100 to expose changing holdout sizes and a system-level recovery diagnostic so that many stoichiometries from one element system cannot masquerade as many independent discoveries.

The most important limitation is upstream of modelling: this is retrospective recovery inside a fixed Materials Project (MP) catalogue. The positive label is an experimental-provenance proxy, the unlabelled class is not a negative class, and the candidate universe consists of already catalogued formulas. No model or metric extension can turn this dataset into evidence of synthesizability or prospective discovery.

A. Current state

A.1 Two ranking products that must remain distinct

The repository has two separate tasks.

  1. The SQLite query path in src/database.jl retrieves stored composition/score rows subject to element, arity and score-threshold filters. rank! in src/ranking.jl orders those rows by either stored score or cosine similarity to one supplied reference. It does not fit a model or evaluate held-out recovery. Its generic tie rule is descending ranking value, descending stored score, then canonical formula.
  2. The MP research path audits and groups a fixed MP snapshot, creates PU holdouts, fits or constructs training-only scorers, ranks a complete candidate pool and evaluates deliberately hidden positive-provenance compositions. This path has its own score-independent tie hash and does not inherit the SQLite stored-score tie breaker.

The public benchmark_rankings path in src/benchmark.jl is a third, narrower interface for an explicitly binary-labelled pool. It already reports fixed-budget hits, precision, recall, enrichment and simple coverage proxies. Its contract correctly warns that it is inappropriate when “0” merely means unobserved.

A.2 Composition, candidate and positive definitions

Composition in src/compositions.jl accepts simple formulas with valid element symbols and positive integer amounts. It combines repeated symbols, reduces the amounts by their greatest common divisor and alphabetically orders the symbols. Equality and hashing therefore collapse element permutations and proportional spellings such as MgZn, Zn2Mg2 and Mg2Zn2.

For the MP workflow, audit_mp_snapshot in src/mp_audit.jl first verifies the snapshot metadata and checks that records.jsonl normalises to records.tsv. It then groups every polymorph/record by canonical reduced composition. Within a group:

  • positive means at least one record has theoretical=false;
  • unlabelled means every record has theoretical=true;
  • unresolved means no positive record exists and at least one flag is missing.

The fixed real snapshot records 24,020 MP records, 12,506 canonical compositions, 5,359 positives, 7,147 unlabelled compositions, no unresolved compositions and 1,343 mixed true/false groups. The scope is oxygen plus exactly two other elements; it is not an oxidation-state-validated oxide set. These counts are in reports/local/mp-ternary-audit/audit.toml and summarised in docs/recovery-findings.md.

In mp_recovery_splits (src/mp_recovery.jl), a composition-holdout candidate pool is the union of a hash-selected fifth of positive compositions and every eligible unlabelled composition. Training contains the remaining positives. For the original policy, each of the 20 real splits has 4,288 training positives, 1,071 held-out positives and 8,218 candidates.

In system_splits (src/mp_system_holdout.jl), a chemical system is the sorted set of element symbols, ignoring stoichiometry. A hash-selected fifth of the eligible system universe supplies candidates: all held-out positives and unlabelled formulas in those systems. Training positives come only from other systems. Depending on label policy and seed, the real system-holdout pools have 2,096–2,710 candidates and 740–1,171 held-out positives.

Thus a candidate is an eligible, already catalogued canonical composition in the evaluator-owned pool. It is not a formula newly generated by Eka. A recommendation is a candidate selected from the top of a complete deterministic ranking. A relevant item is a deliberately held-out positive-provenance composition under the active label policy. There are hundreds or thousands of relevant items per split, not one relevant item per query.

A.3 Filtering and label isolation

Filtering occurs before ranking and is explicit:

  • the API snapshot query requires oxygen, exactly three elements, non-deprecated records and include_gnome=false;
  • the audit excludes invalid/nonintegral formulas and anything outside scope;
  • unresolved groups are outside training and evaluation;
  • the selected label policy either retains, excludes or unlabels mixed groups;
  • system holdout keeps candidate formulas only in selected systems, so eligible unlabelled formulas in other systems are deliberately unused;
  • no post-score threshold removes candidates, and budgets are rejected rather than truncated when K exceeds the pool;
  • the element-pair model retains cold candidates with score zero and an explicit coverage flag instead of dropping them.

pu_rank accepts training formulas, candidate formulas, method and seeds, but no labels, IDs or stored scores. pu_metrics receives held-out labels only after the ranking exists. Tests in test/test_mp_pu.jl, test/test_mp_label_sensitivity.jl and test/test_mp_system_holdout.jl check label isolation, refitting, membership disjointness, deterministic order and tamper rejection.

A.4 Methods already implemented

The PU workflow already includes:

  • a deterministic SHA-256 pseudorandom ranking plus an exact uniform-random expected hit count;
  • training-element popularity, counting each element once per unique training composition;
  • maximum cosine similarity between a candidate's reduced stoichiometric vector and any training composition;
  • a rank-four symmetric nonnegative element-pair factor model in src/element_pair_model.jl.

The learned model constructs a 117×117 matrix over non-oxygen elements. Each training composition increments its unordered non-O pair. It fits nonnegative factors to log-scaled counts with weight 1 on observed pairs, weight 0.01 on unobserved pairs, L2 regularisation 0.01 and deterministic projected-gradient descent. Candidate score is the factor dot product for its non-O pair. All stoichiometries in the same chemical system therefore receive the same model score.

The model is not a Seko reproduction. Its missing-pair penalty is a disclosed implicit-feedback assumption, not a claim that missing compounds are negative. The primary 2,000-iteration study had 101 capped fits out of 120. A later 20,000-iteration stability run converged all fits; 55 formerly capped top-100 sets changed, with up to 48 replacements, although hit deltas averaged +0.16 and ranged from -5 to +4. The frozen 2,000-iteration result remains primary.

A.5 Results already established

The completed comparisons make several proposed “future” tasks unnecessary:

  • Under original composition holdout at K=100, maximum similarity exceeded popularity by only 0.50 mean hits across 20 overlapping splits.
  • Under original system holdout, similarity trailed popularity by 14.45 mean hits at K=100.
  • The element-pair model was essentially tied with popularity under original composition holdout (-0.20 mean hits) and exceeded it under original system holdout (+7.95 mean hits).
  • Label-policy branches, all four budgets and unfavorable outcomes are retained.
  • Post-hoc diagnostics show that the original system-holdout model's top 100 contained about 13.5 systems, about 9 positive-hit systems and only about 5.68 effective hit systems on average. Composition-level Hits@100 therefore does not represent 100 independent chemical decisions.

These are descriptive results over overlapping repeated holdouts. The repository correctly makes no confidence-interval, significance, synthesis-success or causal claim from them.

A.6 Validation and provenance already present

The project is unusually strong here. Frozen protocol documents are content-hash pinned in src/mp_recovery.jl. Split and run bundles retain source/input hashes, membership hashes, implementation hashes, configuration, seeds, Julia/package versions, complete rankings and metric denominators. Python analyzers independently reconstruct membership, baseline scores, ordering and metrics. Separate-checkout reruns reproduced deterministic outputs; runtime files are explicitly excluded from byte equality.

Real snapshots, record-level rankings, factors and complete environments remain under ignored data/local/ and reports/local/ paths. This preserves source release hygiene but means a public checkout cannot exactly reproduce the real results without the separately retained inputs.

B. Scientific task definition

B.1 Prediction task

For each outer split s, let:

  • T_s be the canonical positive-provenance training compositions;
  • C_s be the complete eligible candidate pool;
  • R_s ⊂ C_s be the deliberately hidden positive-provenance compositions;
  • f_m(c; T_s) be method m's score using only permitted training information;
  • π_ms be the deterministic complete ordering of C_s.

The task is: rank C_s so that members of R_s appear early. This is masked-entry recovery from a positive–unlabelled catalogue. It is not binary classification, candidate generation, crystal-structure prediction, stability prediction or prospective synthesis recommendation.

The relevant set is R_s. It is complete with respect to the positives deliberately masked by the split, but it is not complete with respect to all scientifically viable materials because the unlabelled pool can contain viable compounds.

B.2 Natural evaluation unit

The natural statistical/reporting unit is an outer split and its ranked candidate pool. The ranked item is a canonical composition. There is no natural per-user or per-reference “query,” so query-averaged recommender metrics should not be imported by analogy.

Chemical system is an important secondary unit because formulas within a system are clustered and the element-pair model assigns them identical scores. Report both composition recovery and system recovery; do not silently replace one with the other because they answer different screening questions.

B.3 Meaningful budgets

Retain K = 20, 50, 100 and 200 for continuity with every frozen study. K=100 is the primary fixed screening budget. The smaller and larger budgets describe the recovery curve and cutoff sensitivity. They are not percentiles: K=100 is about 1.2% of an original composition-holdout pool but roughly 3.7–4.8% of a system holdout pool. Consequently, compare methods within the same split first; comparisons between split designs must show pool size and prevalence.

If a system-ranked diagnostic is added, choose its system budgets from pre-score population counts and a stated experimental decision capacity, then freeze them. Do not choose them after viewing method performance.

C. Gaps

  1. Metrics are embedded in workflow code. pu_metrics returns only the existing composition-level fixed-budget fields. There is no reusable relevance metric type, system-level evaluator or single schema shared by the binary and PU paths.
  2. Clustered recovery is not a first-class metric. Effective/unique hit-system summaries exist only in a post-hoc Python diagnostic. A method can receive many composition hits from a few systems, especially when its score is constant within system.
  3. The learned representation lacks its most direct control. The factor model is compared with element popularity and composition similarity, but not with the raw training pair count that it factorises. That control is necessary to show whether low-rank completion adds value beyond memorising observed pairs.
  4. The missing-pair assumption is unablated. Weight 0.01 is a major modelling choice. Current code rejects zero, so an observed-pairs-only ablation would be a new, prospectively frozen experiment.
  5. Optimisation status complicates the headline model result. Most primary fits hit the 2,000-iteration cap, and many rankings changed at 20,000 iterations. The stability evidence is good, but any next model comparison should use a convergence rule or fixed-compute claim defined before scoring.
  6. The primary split should be stated more strongly. Composition grouping blocks exact duplicates and polymorph leakage, but composition holdout permits same-system stoichiometries and close analogues in training. System holdout is the more defensible headline for transfer to unseen element combinations.
  7. No protocol tests a genuinely new material or time period. The snapshot lacks defensible first-discovery timestamps, and all candidates already occur in MP. Chronological or prospective claims are unavailable.
  8. Provenance is comprehensive but not uniform. Core result configs do not consistently store a Git commit plus dirty-tree digest, Python environment, OS/architecture/CPU, thread counts or one common result schema. Source hashes are stronger than a commit alone, but restoration currently depends on experiment-specific packaging.
  9. Performance evidence is split across scripts and reports. There are useful measurements but no isolated benchmark environment, scaling grid or end-to-end phase timings. Cumulative allocations are sometimes available; peak resident memory is not.
  10. Public reproducibility is deliberately incomplete. Real data and detailed outputs are local pending artifact review. Public claims must stay within the aggregate evidence that can be shared and attributed.

D. Recommended evaluation design

D.1 Primary protocol: repeated chemical-system holdout

Use the existing v2 definition as the foundation: hash-select whole canonical element sets, fit on positive compositions outside those systems, and rank all eligible held-out-positive plus unlabelled formulas inside the selected systems. This supports the narrow claim:

recovery of MP positive-provenance compositions when their exact element combination is absent from positive training data.

It prevents exact composition leakage, proportional/permuted formula leakage, polymorph leakage and same-element-system stoichiometric leakage. It does not prevent transfer through chemically analogous systems, periodic-family neighbours or each element's occurrence with other partners. That remaining information is intentional for the element-pair task and must be stated.

Retain the 20 deterministic split seeds for continuity, but treat them as overlapping sensitivity analyses, not independent replicates. Report every split. Use macro means/medians across splits as the main aggregate so each split receives equal weight. A pooled micro recall can be supplied only as a labelled diagnostic; it weights splits with more held-out compositions more heavily and should not replace macro summaries.

D.2 Interpolation control: repeated composition holdout

Retain the existing composition holdout as a secondary control. It simulates recovering missing formulas when other stoichiometries or formulas in the same element system may already be known. It is scientifically useful for database completion and for quantifying how much a method depends on local analogues. It does not support a claim of transfer to unseen chemistry.

D.3 Optional later stress test: leave-one-element-out

If a cold-start question becomes important, prospectively define a leave-one-nonoxygen-element-out protocol over elements with adequate positive and unlabelled support. Training must exclude every formula containing the held-out element; candidates contain that element. This tests transfer to an unseen element, not merely an unseen pair. It is expected to expose the element-pair model's documented zero-score fallback and is valuable as a limitation test, not as a route to a favorable score.

Do not call this “leave-one-chemical-family-out.” The repository has no validated chemical-family taxonomy. Do not add a chronological split until a source with defensible observation/discovery dates is obtained; MP snapshot or creation timestamps are not sufficient.

D.5 Primary metrics

Use the smallest set that answers the screening question and keeps changing denominators visible.

  1. Composition Hits@100. Number of held-out positive-provenance formulas in the first 100. This is the primary operational measure. At a fixed screening budget it has a direct meaning and preserves all existing comparisons.
  2. Composition Recall@100. Hits@100 divided by the split's held-out positive count. This is necessary when system-holdout relevant-set sizes differ. Call it “held-out recovery recall,” not sensitivity to true viable materials.
  3. Paired ΔHits@100. Within each identical split/candidate pool, report the method-minus-popularity difference and method-minus-raw-pair-frequency difference. This is the primary comparative statistic rather than a standalone retrieval metric. Retain every split, mean, median, range and sign counts.

Always show N, |R|, prevalence and the analytical uniform-random expected hits beside these metrics. Precision@100 is algebraically Hits@100/100 and need not be a separate headline number; the current name observed_label_fraction is scientifically safer because unlabelled is not negative.

D.6 Secondary diagnostic metrics

  • Hits and recall at K = 20, 50 and 200 to show budget sensitivity.
  • Observed-label enrichment over split prevalence. This helps compare pools with different prevalence but does not correct selection bias in the catalogue.
  • Distinct positive systems@K, system recall@K (held-out systems with at least one recovered formula / held-out systems) and the already used effective hit-system count. These expose clustered hits without changing the composition screening budget.
  • A separately ranked system diagnostic, using one prospectively defined aggregate score per system and deterministic system ties, only if the scientific decision is genuinely system-level.
  • Binary NDCG@K as an optional position-sensitive diagnostic. With binary held-out relevance it asks whether recovered positives occur near the very top, and its ideal denominator is well defined per split. It should not replace Hits@K.
  • Score coverage/cold-candidate count, distinct-score count, tie-block sizes and whether a tie crosses K.
  • Candidate-to-training similarity distributions and the share of candidates sharing systems with training; these diagnose split difficulty rather than model quality.
  • Unique selected systems, effective selected systems and element coverage for list-composition diagnostics. Call them diversity/coverage proxies, not chemical diversity or novelty.

Do not add MRR: the first recovered item discards almost all information when hundreds of positives are relevant. Do not add hit rate: “at least one hit” will usually saturate at these budgets. Do not make MAP primary: there is one global ranking per split rather than many user/query rankings, and average precision implicitly gives meaning to the complete rank tail. Do not report exact-formula novelty because every held-out candidate is composition-disjoint by construction. NDCG and MAP also inherit the incomplete-ground-truth limitation of every PU metric; an unlabelled formula can be scientifically valuable while counted as nonrelevant.

D.7 Ties, duplicates and denominators

  • Preserve exact score equality. Do not round or use tolerance bins before ordering.
  • Use the existing score-independent SHA-256 tie key and canonical formula fallback for PU composition rankings. Record the tie seed and algorithm ID.
  • If system rankings are added, derive a separate versioned system tie key from the canonical system string. Do not let the number of formulas in a system decide ties accidentally.
  • Reject duplicate canonical candidates before scoring. Do not deduplicate a ranked output after the fact.
  • Define N and R after all protocol eligibility rules but before scoring. Invalid, out-of-scope, unresolved and policy-excluded groups are audit counts, not metric denominators. Under system holdout, eligible formulas in unselected systems are also outside that split's denominator.
  • Never drop an unscorable candidate. Use an explicit fallback/coverage state and keep it in N, as the element-pair implementation already does.
  • When exclusion changes eligibility, compact ranks before applying K and record the original rank depth, following the existing label-sensitivity implementation.

E. PyTorch decision

Decision: defer.

The current learned problem has only 117 element rows and rank four. Reimplementing the same weighted symmetric factor objective in PyTorch would mainly compare an optimizer/autodiff stack with Julia's explicit projected-gradient implementation; it would not add a scientifically distinct predictor. The measured primary run spent about 18.71 seconds fitting all 120 branches and 0.83 seconds ranking them, so accelerator support is not needed. A neural collaborative-filtering scorer or MLP would require a new representation and a defensible PU loss/negative-sampling policy. Adding one merely to claim PyTorch would be disconnected from the current evidence.

Reconsider PyTorch only after both of these prerequisites hold:

  1. the system-aware evaluation and raw-pair control identify a specific remaining error mode, such as inability to order stoichiometries within a system; and
  2. a representation with information capable of addressing that error is available without test leakage—for example training-only composition descriptors or structure/property features with complete provenance.

The smallest credible later experiment would keep the exact outer splits, candidate universe, held-out positives, filters, tie policy and metric code. It would use one shallow model over the agreed representation, a prospectively defined PU objective, training-only negative/unlabelled sampling, deterministic CPU execution first, fixed seeds, inner grouped validation for early stopping and hyperparameters, and TSV rankings plus TOML/JSON provenance consumable by Julia and the independent Python validator. GPU use would require recording device, driver, framework determinism settings and nondeterministic operators. Until the loss and representation are scientifically specified, optimizer, learning rate, regularisation and negative-sampling counts should not be invented.

F.1 Existing empirical evidence

  • The 12-row fixture measurement reduced first in-process query latency from about 142 ms to 6 ms after representative precompilation; warm-query differences were too small to interpret. This is startup evidence, not an algorithmic speedup.
  • A production legacy query returned 1,108 rows in roughly 104–106 ms warm. A streaming validation scan of 4,736,551 rows took about 39 seconds in one exploratory run.
  • The synthetic real-size similarity benchmark scored about 35.2 million candidate/training pairs in 0.132 s and allocated 3.2 MiB; full pu_rank similarity took 0.141 s and 21.8 MiB. A stored Float64 pairwise matrix would be 268.9 MiB. These are cumulative allocations, not peak RSS.
  • In the retained real pilot, similarity ranking totaled about 2.90 s across 20 splits, versus 0.45 s for popularity and 0.37 s for random, including first-call compilation effects.
  • The 120 primary pair fits totaled 18.71 s; ranking totaled 0.83 s. Mean cumulative fit allocation was about 75 MiB for composition branches and 72 MiB for system branches. Input verification, output, analysis, startup and reproduction were not included in those totals.

F.2 Next benchmark design

Create a separate benchmark/Project.toml with BenchmarkTools so benchmarking dependencies do not enter the package runtime. Add:

  • microbenchmarks for parsing, tie-key construction, pu_metrics, raw pair score, factor objective/gradient and sorting at controlled tie rates;
  • scaling benchmarks for N, T, E and r, reporting median time, allocations and fitted slopes against the theoretical terms;
  • end-to-end phase timers for verify/audit, split construction, fitting, scoring, sorting, metric computation and output;
  • OS-reported peak RSS in a fresh process when making memory claims;
  • machine, OS/architecture, Julia, dependency, thread-count, dataset hash and commit/dirty-state metadata.

Profile before optimizing. Likely targets are SHA string allocation in ranking, MP audit's full-file duplication and broadcast temporaries in projected gradient, but none should be called a bottleneck until a profiler on the intended workload shows it.

Defensible claims after the proposed measurements include “streamed maximum similarity with O(T+N) working storage,” “deterministic O(N log N) ranking,” and a specific measured reduction in time/allocations on a named workload. Do not claim generic “faster ranking,” lower peak memory from @allocated, production-scale speedup from the 12-row fixture, or asymptotic improvement from a constant-factor change.

Reusable package code should calculate metrics and scores from in-memory canonical objects. Research orchestration should verify sources, rebuild split-local state, write complete artifacts and refuse overwrite. Python should independently read the saved schema and recompute results; it should not call Julia metric code. Plots and narrative tables belong to generated analysis output, not src/. Notebooks are unnecessary for the core workflow.

Avoid modifying fields in existing schema-v1 reports. Define a new result schema with, at minimum:

  • dataset ID/version/retrieval metadata and hashes;
  • audit/preprocessing algorithm and implementation hashes;
  • code commit, dirty flag, patch/source-tree digest and relevant file hashes;
  • protocol ID/hash, split algorithm, split membership hashes and label policy;
  • candidate-generation/filter definition and exclusion counts;
  • method/baseline/ablation configuration, seed roles and coverage policy;
  • metric algorithm/version, K values, grouping rule and denominator counts;
  • Julia/Python versions, dependency locks, OS, architecture, CPU, threads and optional accelerator details;
  • hashes for every deterministic output, with runtime/host measurements clearly separated.

A deterministic run ID can be the SHA-256 of the canonical scientific configuration and input identities. Wall-clock timestamps and output paths must not enter that identity.

H.1 Required cross-cutting tests

Metrics

  • Hand-compute Hits, Recall, observed-label fraction, enrichment, DCG/NDCG, distinct hit systems, system recall and effective systems.
  • Cover K=1, K=N, K>N, unsorted K input and duplicate K.
  • Reject empty candidate rankings, duplicate canonical candidates, duplicate relevance, relevance outside the candidate universe and empty relevance unless a future API explicitly defines nullable denominators. Preserve pu_metrics's current strict behavior for compatibility.
  • Test all-zero relevance scores, all-relevant rankings, no top-K hits and ties spanning K.
  • Verify metric values do not depend on candidate input order once a ranking is supplied, and that metric computation does not mutate inputs.
  • Compare Julia with an independent Python implementation on golden fixtures; include integer/rational expected-random values to avoid floating ambiguity.

Splits and leakage

  • Assert train/candidate composition disjointness and, for the primary protocol, train/candidate chemical-system disjointness.
  • Include proportional formulas, symbol permutations, polymorph records, mixed flags, unlabelled-only systems, unsupported records and unequal system sizes.
  • Assert deterministic membership under input and seed-list permutation.
  • Change evaluator labels and unrelated candidate membership while holding training fixed; fitted state and shared-candidate scores must not change.
  • If inner validation is introduced, assert outer candidates never appear in inner training, preprocessing, sampling or selection.

Baselines and ablation

  • Check popularity and pair counts on hand-worked examples.
  • Prove raw pair frequency is zero for every candidate in a valid system holdout.
  • Verify maximum similarity against direct pairwise similarity and a Python calculation on a small fixture.
  • Verify alpha=0 changes only the missing-pair weights/configuration; observed targets, regularisation, initialization, stopping rule and membership must match.
  • Test complete finite score coverage and explicit cold/unseen states.

Ranking and reproducibility

  • Golden-test split, random and tie SHA keys, including exact-score tie blocks at K and synthetic hash-collision fallback logic where feasible.
  • Reject duplicate candidates rather than silently retaining one.
  • Re-run with reversed inputs and perturbed global RNG state.
  • Validate result schema types, required fields, deterministic run ID and every output checksum; reject semantically corrupted membership/results even after checksums are rewritten.
  • Compare two same-environment synthetic runs byte for byte except declared runtime/host fields.

Performance

  • Keep correctness fixtures small. Put realistic scaling and allocation checks in the isolated benchmark suite, not the unit suite.
  • Record compilation-inclusive and warmed measurements separately.
  • Use a fresh process for peak RSS and end-to-end latency.
  • Verify any optimization preserves complete ranking digests, not only top K.

I. Risks and open questions

  1. Scientific target: Is the goal database completion, unseen-system transfer or actual experimental recommendation? The current data answer only the first two, using a provenance proxy.
  2. Candidate universe: There is no enumerator for absent formulas. Ranking already catalogued MP compositions cannot measure prospective discovery among genuinely unknown candidates.
  3. Ground truth: theoretical=false is not literature-audited synthesis success; mixed groups show label ambiguity. Unlabelled is not failure.
  4. Within-system dependence: The main model ignores stoichiometry and generates very large tie blocks. Composition hits can be concentrated in a few systems.
  5. Optimisation: The frozen primary model often stopped at its iteration cap. A future protocol must choose convergence handling before results, not promote the favorable checkpoint afterward.
  6. Split dependence: The 20 hash holdouts overlap. They quantify sensitivity to membership but do not create independent experimental replicates.
  7. Cross-design confounding: Existing system and composition pools differ in training size, candidate size and prevalence. Without a prospectively defined matched control, protocol differences are descriptive, not causal effects of chemical separation.
  8. Chemical OOD: System disjointness does not prevent close periodic-family analogues. A family split needs an external, justified taxonomy.
  9. Time: The snapshot has retrieval time but no defensible first-observation time. Chronological evaluation is currently unavailable.
  10. Distribution and reproducibility: Aggregate attributed results may be shareable, but exact public reruns still depend on local record-level artifacts and final permissions review.

Final direction

The best next investment is not a larger model. It is a small evaluation kernel that makes system-level concentration impossible to overlook, followed by the raw pair-frequency control. If those results show that the factor model's apparent system-holdout advantage survives system-aware evaluation and truly comes from low-rank cross-pair generalisation, a richer stoichiometric model becomes a well-posed next question. If they do not, the negative result is scientifically more valuable than adding PyTorch or tuning rank until one branch improves.
