# Eka Materials Selection: One-Page Charter

11 October 2026. Owner: Joshua Corbett. Stage: solo-built MVP before first user.

**Purpose.** Give engineers a quick point of reference for choosing among existing
materials. Turn a design brief into a small set of plausible options with clear
pros and cons, supporting evidence, and sourcing links. Make the same capability
available to other engineering agents.

**Users.** Engineers in smaller firms and consultancies are the initial audience.
Engineers and materials specialists in larger firms may use the same tool to
accelerate reference searches and comparisons. The first individual user and
industry are still to be identified; neither is a condition of starting.

**Decision supported.** Which existing material families or commercial grades
deserve consideration, what tradeoffs distinguish them, and what needs checking
next? The engineer retains design and qualification decisions. Distinguish
family-level exploration, grade-specific evidence, and purchasable products.

**Application and geography.** General materials selection across engineering is
the ambition. No ceramics-only vertical or UK/EU market is committed. The MVP
uses a small declared set of examples and sources chosen for feasibility. Region,
manufacturing, and supply requirements can be task inputs; coverage remains visible.

**First experience.** Describe a problem, inspect and correct the requirements
Eka extracts, and receive approximately three to five options where evidence
permits. Each option shows advantages, disadvantages, property values and
conditions, citations, sourcing links, and unresolved questions. Adjust the
requirements and compare again.

**Product form.** A comparison view and MCP interface are useful initial options.
The eventual balance between a standalone application and a tool used by other
agents is open. Evidence and comparison functions should serve both.

**MVP boundary.** Build enough of this workflow to approach one engineer with
something usable. Use a modest catalogue and truthful coverage. A funded pilot,
hired reviewer, several recruited firms, and formal benchmarking come later if
useful. Basic source, unit, grade-identity, and constraint checks belong in the
MVP; they do not constitute independent engineering validation.

**Exclusions.** Novel material generation, predictions presented as measurements,
autonomous design approval, exhaustive coverage claims, automatic supplier
contact/purchasing, and enterprise deployment. Choosing existing-material
selection does not require claiming that generative research is useful only in
certain industries.

**Success now.** A runnable demo handles a few representative briefs, produces
traceable comparisons, exposes missing evidence, and lets an engineer challenge
the result. Next, one engineer tries a real task and identifies what helps and
what needs correction. Time savings, broad accuracy, and willingness to pay
remain hypotheses.
