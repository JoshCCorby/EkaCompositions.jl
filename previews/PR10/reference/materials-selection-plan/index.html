Eka Materials Selection: MVP to First User

Revised 13 October 2026 following Joshua's clarification. This replaces the 8 October pilot-first sequence, retained in Git at 2ea4459. Its recruitment, reviewer, budget, and benchmark targets are not current prerequisites. The first three tasks are complete: demonstration briefs, source inventory, and the isolated evidence/storage service. The code currently uses synthetic fixtures; recommendations and the interface are not built.

Direction

Build a quick, inspectable materials reference for engineering firms, consultants, and materials specialists. An engineer describes a design problem; Eka proposes existing materials, explains their pros and cons, and links to the evidence and sourcing options. Other engineering agents can use the same service.

Joshua is the sole builder. The immediate objective is a working MVP to show a potential first user. Engineer feedback will help determine the final product form. A funded pilot, hired reviewer, interviews, and a fixed industry niche are not entry requirements.

The long-term ambition is broad search across known materials. The MVP will disclose the sources and material classes actually searched. A limited catalogue cannot support a claim of searching every known material, and an empty result cannot establish that no suitable material exists.

These are repository-local task identifiers. Joshua owns all work; Codex can assist with implementation and board maintenance. Completion criteria and artifact targets are in the backlog. The task board records actual progress; this roadmap does not imply the full product already exists.

1. Define a small demonstration

Write three illustrative engineering briefs. They can be self-authored and must be labelled as demonstrations, not customer research or an engineering benchmark. Choose them against available evidence so they are feasible to implement.

Possible examples are a lightweight bracket, a temperature-exposed enclosure, and an electrically insulating component that must transfer heat. These are proposals, not commitments to particular applications or materials. Include incomplete requirements and competing priorities.

Keep the input model general. If practical, cover more than one material family; do not force breadth when reliable comparisons are missing. The initial inventory now identifies four reference candidates; the former 15-30-grade estimate is not a starting quota. No manufacturer records are approved for reuse yet.

Build a source register: manufacturer, document URL, grade coverage, revision and access date, acquisition method, storage/display permissions, and gaps. Use accessible, permitted sources initially. Public visibility does not establish republication rights. Synthetic records can unblock implementation while real data access is unresolved.

Done when: three briefs and a small source set support an end-to-end demo. No recruited engineer is needed to start.

2. Establish the evidence model

Use separate identifiers for material families, manufacturer grades, product forms, observations, sources, and supplier listings. Composition is optional metadata; it does not uniquely identify a commercial grade.

Each observation needs its value/range, units, applicable conditions, test method where stated, typical versus specified-limit status, evidence type, source revision, and exact page/table or other location. Preserve missing facts and conflicting observations. Do not merge different grades or conditions.

Manually check the initial decisive observations. Automated extraction can propose entries, but ambiguous headers, units, and footnotes need resolution before the values qualify a candidate. Publish a versioned catalogue.

Keep the Julia research package and frozen protocols intact. The new service lives under services/materials-selection/. MVP-03 established Python, Pydantic, Pint, and SQLite; the MCP adapter is later work. Do not introduce a Julia/Python runtime bridge unless a specific reusable capability justifies it.

Done when: each decisive demo value resolves to a grade, condition, and source; unchanged inputs reproduce the catalogue identity.

3. Make comparisons work

Implement grade/alias lookup and structured property filters first. Evaluate hard requirements before preferences. Each constraint returns pass, fail, unknown, or conflicting evidence with the observations and rule used.

Check units, temperature, processing state, direction, and evidence requirements before comparing values. A typical value cannot establish a guaranteed minimum. Unsupported extrapolation and missing conditions remain unknown.

Show alternatives and the reasons to choose or reject them. Expose where improving one preference sacrifices another. Use visible weights only when useful; never let a preference score cancel a failed hard constraint. Separate provisional candidates from supported matches.

Link exact grades/products to manufacturers or suppliers. Show quantity, region, price basis, availability, and date only when documented. A product page supports a sourcing route; it does not prove stock or volume capacity. Accept geographic preferences without imposing UK/EU as the product's market. Supplier outreach, quotations, and purchasing remain outside this MVP.

Done when: structured requests produce reproducible comparisons and sourcing links or explicit sourcing gaps without an LLM.

4. Add the agent workflow

Translate a brief into function, environment, geometry, manufacturing, supplied limits, preferences, and unknowns. Separate user statements from model suggestions and derived requirements. Ask questions likely to change the comparison.

Initially collect and clarify engineering limits rather than inferring detailed physics from vague descriptions. Add a numerical derivation only when its formula, inputs, units, and validity assumptions are explicit and tested. Let the engineer correct the proposed specification.

Have the model call the comparison engine and explain its outputs. The evidence store supplies values; deterministic rules perform numerical checks. Retrieved content is data, not instructions. A multi-agent scientist or autonomous research loop is not necessary for the first version.

Return roughly three to five options when supported, with advantages, disadvantages, decisive evidence, unknowns, and sourcing links. Return fewer or an insufficient-evidence result when appropriate. Include coverage and assumptions that could change the answer.

Done when: a demo brief can be clarified, searched, and explained with every decisive numerical claim grounded in a returned observation.

5. Make it usable

Provide a small local interface: brief, editable requirements, comparison table, and source detail. Open directly into selection. Export the task, tradeoffs, sources, and catalogue version. This is a demonstration surface; the eventual product could become agent-first, browser-first, or both.

Expose the same functions through MCP: validate requirements, search, inspect a grade, compare, and retrieve evidence/sourcing. Use an official SDK, pin a tested release, and start with local stdio. A host agent can prepare the specification without duplicating an internal model call. Handle malformed inputs, absent data, cancellation, and errors. Hosted access and accounts follow actual demand.

Done when: the interface and an MCP client can produce the same comparison without manually repairing the data.

6. Prepare the first-user demo

Check identity, units, boundaries, wrong conditions, conflicting sources, hard failures masked by preferences, unknowns treated as passes, broken citations, and no-match cases. Include out-of-coverage questions and misleading instructions embedded in a source. These are software checks, not application approval.

Run the three demonstration briefs end to end. Record latency, model cost, failures, and manual interventions. Check source links and exports. Test setup from a clean environment with synthetic fixtures. Keep restricted data, private briefs, and credentials out of Git.

Package a runnable build, short setup instructions, and example tasks. A stable local demo that an engineer can inspect and challenge is enough; public launch, a formal benchmark, and multiple recruited firms are not requirements.

Done when: all three demos work, decisive claims are traceable, coverage is visible, and a first engineer can try the workflow.

7. Learn from one engineer

Approach one engineer with the working MVP. Observe a real task and record useful suggestions, missing facts, disputed comparisons, and usability problems. Get permission before retaining or transmitting confidential project information.

Prioritize corrections and another real use. Let experience determine initial applications, coverage, interface, and specialist-review needs. A demonstration does not establish demand, design qualification, or broad accuracy.

A structured pilot, independent review, held-out evaluation, paid data access, hosting, and payment tests become relevant afterward when they answer observed questions. Keep final design decisions with the responsible engineer.

Deferred work

  • Recruiting several firms or hiring a specialist before building.
  • A formal pilot budget, payment targets, and a 20-task held-out benchmark.
  • Complete database aggregation, exhaustive coverage claims, and broad crawling.
  • Generative material design and learned property prediction.
  • Automated qualification, supplier contact, and purchasing.
  • Enterprise accounts, CAD/FEA integrations, and multi-tenant hosting.

Basic source checks, correct comparisons, and honest unknowns belong in the MVP. The deferred work is a later product programme, not a condition of beginning.

Next step

MVP-01 through MVP-03 are complete. Continue with MVP-05's deterministic comparison logic against synthetic fixtures, while MVP-04 establishes a permitted real-data route. The source inventory records the current rights and verification gaps; the service README documents the runnable foundation. Build one complete comparison before expanding. Re-estimate after that first path works. The earlier 12-16 week beta schedule is withdrawn because it assumed a different team and validation sequence.
